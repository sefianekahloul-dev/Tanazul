How buyers will see it

One transfer, checked at every step.

An off-plan resale is a contract assignment (tanazul). The buyer steps into the seller's contract with the developer, so the developer and any lending bank have to approve it.

1

Seller lists the contract

Original price, down payment, installments paid and left, handover date, and an asking price for the equity.

2

We verify the payment record

The developer or bank confirms the statement. Verified listings carry a badge, and the numbers cannot be edited afterwards.

3

Developer issues the NOC

The No Objection Certificate approves the transfer. Bank-financed units also need the bank to agree to transfer or settle the loan.

4

Escrow closes the deal

The buyer's payment sits in escrow until the developer registers the new owner, then it releases to the seller.

Questions

Is transferring an off-plan contract allowed?

Most developers allow a transfer with their No Objection Certificate. Some require a minimum share of the price to be paid first and charge a transfer fee. Each listing shows the developer's rules once confirmed.

Who can see my listing?

Public listings are visible to everyone. Discreet listings hide the project name and photos until you approve a verified buyer.

What if my unit is bank-financed?

The bank must approve the transfer, or the loan is settled at closing from the buyer's payment.

When do I get my money?

The buyer's payment is held in escrow and released to you once the developer registers the new owner.

Can I change my mind?

Yes. You can withdraw your listing at any time before a buyer is confirmed.
